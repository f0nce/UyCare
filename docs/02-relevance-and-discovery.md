# Evidence of relevance and user discovery

## Evidence currently available

E01. The project team supplied a marketplace concept, target country, team roles and the Flutter constraint in the project discussion on 21 October 2026. This establishes project intent, not demand or usability.

E02. Official product descriptions show that request posting, price comparison, reviews and direct selection already exist in relevant systems [S11–S13]. This supports the feasibility of the interaction pattern and the need for a careful comparison. It does not establish a competitive advantage for UyCare.

E03. Research on platform governance and scams motivates explicit responsibilities, review rules and moderation [S01, S02]. Transfer to local repair services is a design inference that needs local validation.

No user interviews, client agreement, observations of a real repair process, or local usage statistics have yet been supplied. The committee requirement for 3–5 discussions or a substantive client agreement remains open. Planned discussions must never be presented as conducted interviews.

## Assumptions to validate

H1. Customers have difficulty comparing repair offers because scope, materials and timing are not recorded consistently. Validate with accounts of recent repair jobs and examples of how quotes were compared.

H2. Portfolios and completed-job reviews affect selection alongside price. Ask participants to rank and explain choices between neutral sample profiles.

H3. Providers will respond to a visible request with their own price and schedule. Ask how they currently find work, what information they need and why they decline jobs.

H4. OSI/KSK representatives need shared-area request ownership and a visible decision history. Validate with an actual representative, including how authority to commission work is established.

H5. Category suggestions reduce confusion without hiding suitable providers. Observe manual category choice and correction of intentionally wrong suggestions.

H6. Customers and providers want in-app communication. Compare their preferences for phone and chat rather than assuming that chat is sufficient.

## Recruitment and protocol

Target five discussions: two residential customers, two providers from different trades, and one OSI/KSK representative. Three is the minimum if all three perspectives are represented. This is a purposive discovery sample, not statistically representative market research. Marsel coordinates recruitment; Kazbek interviews customers and the representative; Daryn interviews providers and records category vocabulary. Recruitment and participation are not yet confirmed.

Each discussion lasts approximately 20–30 minutes. Explain the student project and obtain permission to take notes. Recording requires separate consent. Use participant codes P01–P05. Keep phone numbers, addresses and recordings outside the public repository. Participants may decline a question or withdraw their material before analysis.

Begin with the participant's most recent real repair experience before showing the concept. Ask neutral questions, record counterexamples, and distinguish exact quotations from paraphrases. After discovery, use the prototype for a separate usability exercise. Do not merge the two forms of evidence.

## Interview guide

1. Describe the last time you needed a repair or accepted a repair job. What happened from start to finish?
2. Where did you find the customer or provider, and which alternatives did you consider?
3. What information was missing when you first discussed the work?
4. How were the price, materials, travel cost and timing agreed? What changed later?
5. What helped you trust the other person? What evidence would not be useful?
6. How did you compare alternatives? Describe a case where the cheapest option was not selected.
7. How did you communicate and record completion? Did any misunderstanding occur?
8. What would make you refuse a request or decline to use an online platform?
9. For OSI/KSK representatives: who can publish, select and approve work, and how is that authority recorded?
10. After seeing the prototype: which step matches your process least well, and what would need to change?

## Analysis and acceptance

For each discussion, retain date, participant role, consent status, interviewer, evidence reference, observed workflow, difficulties, counterexamples and proposed requirement changes. Summarise themes with participant codes and frequencies only within the actual sample. A statement such as 3 of 5 participants is descriptive of the sample, not of Kazakhstan.

Each requirement influenced by research receives a link to its evidence code. Marsel and another member review the interpretation. Mandatory requirements are revised when findings contradict assumptions. The evidence component is complete only when at least three real records or a substantive client agreement exist and the resulting design decisions are documented.

The unfilled record form is in research/interview-record-template.md. The evidence register is in research/evidence-register.json. Blank forms and interview questions are preparation materials and do not satisfy the interview requirement.
